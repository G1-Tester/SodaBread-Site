History of Irish Soda Bread

Soda bread emerged in Ireland in the 19th century, when baking soda became widely available and families needed a quick, reliable way to bake bread without yeast.

The traditional loaf was baked in cast-iron pots over the hearth, scored with a cross on top—part practical, part symbolic. It was everyday bread, not a luxury, and its simplicity reflects the resourcefulness of Irish households.
